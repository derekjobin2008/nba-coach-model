Bench Brain

How NBA head coaches manage timeouts, substitutions, and foul trouble, compared with an average coach in the same game situations.

How to read this
  • "In the same situations." Every comparison is against what an average NBA coach would have done in this coach's exact game situations: same score, clock, fouls, timeouts left, what just happened, and (for substitutions) the same players' minutes and fouls. A coach who trails a lot is not counted as "timeout-happy" just for trailing.
  • Substitutions account for the roster. Stars rarely come out of games under any coach, so who a coach has is separated from how he uses them.
  • Usual habit and One season are 95% ranges. "Usual habit" is how precisely the data pins down his normal tendency, including how hard it is to separate him from his team and players; if it does not cross the average line, he is clearly different from an average coach. "One season" is where a single season of his should land, which is wider because coaches really do change from year to year (about 11% for timeouts, 19% for substitutions). Tested on four past seasons, about 95% of coaches' seasons landed inside it.
  • Foul trouble compares the coach's real habits with the same rotation if he ignored fouls, in a model of the game that measures each choice in win probability. It only counts how he handles foul trouble, not how many minutes he gives starters in general.
Method

Data: every regular-season play-by-play from the NBA's public stats site, turned into decision events and game states in SQL (DuckDB). Timeouts and substitutions: a league-wide logistic regression for each decision, plus partially pooled coach, coach-team, and (for substitutions) player effects, fit by penalized maximum likelihood. The models beat the league-average model on a fully held-out season (2025-26). Ranges combine a joint Laplace approximation over all coach, team, and player effects, a game-clustered correction for decisions within a game moving together, and an estimate of real season-to-season change in each coach. A rolling-origin check (fit on earlier seasons, predict each of 2022-23 to 2025-26) confirmed the ranges contain about 95% of coaches' next-season behavior; the simpler range used before contained only 67%.

Foul trouble: a Markov decision process for one starter (minutes left, fouls, minutes played, score margin), solved by backward induction against a win-probability model calibrated to the data. Each coach's on-court shares by quarter and foul count are run through it for a starter worth what his starters are worth over his bench (regularized adjusted plus-minus). Ranges come from resampling his games. Coaches with fewer than 100 close games are not audited.
